# CISSP (Certified Information Systems Security Professional)

Issued by ISC2, the CISSP is the reference certification for senior security roles. It validates that you can design, run and manage a security program across an organization, not just configure a tool.

## Who it is for

- Security managers, architects and consultants
- Experienced IT professionals moving toward security leadership
- Anyone who needs a credential recognized by employers and government bodies (it is accepted under DoD 8140, formerly 8570)

## Requirements

**Experience:** 5 years of cumulative paid work in at least 2 of the 8 CISSP domains.
A 4-year reduction is possible with an approved degree or certification.

**Exam:** computerized adaptive test (CAT) in English, 100 to 150 questions, 3 hours, passing score 700 out of 1000.

**Endorsement:** after passing, an ISC2 member must endorse your application.

## The 8 domains

1. Security and Risk Management
2. Asset Security
3. Security Architecture and Engineering
4. Communication and Network Security
5. Identity and Access Management (IAM)
6. Security Assessment and Testing
7. Security Operations
8. Software Development Security

Domain 1 carries the most weight in the exam outline, so it is worth studying early.

## Maintenance

- 120 CPE (Continuing Professional Education) credits every 3 years
- Annual Maintenance Fee (AMF) to keep the certification active

## Why it matters

- Frequently required for security manager, CISO and architect roles
- Broad scope: it covers governance, risk, engineering and operations, so it complements narrower technical certifications such as OSCP or CompTIA Security+

## Common alternatives

- **CompTIA Security+**: entry point, more technical, no experience requirement
- **CISM**: governance and program management, aimed at managers
- **CISA**: audit and control, aimed at auditors

Costs, exam format and experience rules change. Check the official ISC2 site before registering.
