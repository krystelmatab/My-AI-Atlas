WHERE IT CAME FROM

The word itself

The term was coined in early 2025 by Andrej Karpathy (former Tesla AI director, OpenAI co-founder). His original idea: let go completely, "fully give in to the vibes," let the AI write, and barely look at the code.

In 2025, "vibe coding" was even named Word of the Year by Collins Dictionary.

What it means in 2026

⚠

The meaning has shifted. It's no longer "I let go of everything and trust it blindly." Today, only 29% of developers say they trust the generated code, even though 92% use it daily. The result: serious vibe coding still keeps a human reviewing, testing, and validating.

RELATED CONCEPTS TO KNOW

The vocabulary around vibe coding

Flow state
The state of frictionless focus where ideas flow one into the next. Vibe coding aims to keep you there by removing tedious mechanical tasks.
AI-accelerated development
A more disciplined version: you use AI heavily, but the code still follows the same standards, tests, and review as hand-written code.
Agentic engineering
Autonomous AI agents that edit multiple files, carry out entire tasks, and sometimes even monitor and fix a live app in production on their own.
Vibe & Verify
The workflow that dominates in 2026: generate by prompt, then test and fix before calling it done. "Verify" is no longer optional.
Prompt engineering
The art of phrasing your request well so the AI gets it right the first time. It's become a skill of its own for vibe coders.
Vibe environment
A complete ecosystem that connects code generation to your everyday tools (Jira, Slack, Figma) and scans for security automatically.
Vibe researching
The same principle applied to research: co-driving a literature review or an experiment with AI, instead of code.
KEY TAKEAWAY

The important point

Vibe coding was never meant to replace developers. In real enterprise projects in 2026, someone still has to act as the architect: set the system's boundaries, check security, and review the output like an expert — not just accept whatever the AI proposes.
