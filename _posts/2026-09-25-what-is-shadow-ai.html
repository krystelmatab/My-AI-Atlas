THE BASIC IDEA

AI at work… that nobody approved

Shadow AI = employees using AI tools the company doesn't know about. It's the AI cousin of "shadow IT" (any software used at work without IT's OK).

In the lightAn AI tool the company chose. IT can see it and protect it.
In the shadowA personal AI account. Nobody knows what goes into it.
2023

No single inventor. The phrase popped up in 2023, when people started pasting work into ChatGPT. It borrows from "shadow IT", an older phrase IT teams used for years about software employees installed on their own.

WHY IT HAPPENS

Nobody is trying to be a villain

People just want to get their work done. Three simple reasons:

It's faster
A report in 5 minutes instead of 2 hours.
It's one click away
Free, no ticket, no approval, no waiting.
No official tool
The company hasn't offered one (yet). So people bring their own.
78%
of people who use AI at work bring their own tools. Almost 4 out of 5.Microsoft & LinkedIn survey, 2024, 31,000 workers in 31 countries
THE JOURNEY OF ONE COPY-PASTE

Where your data actually goes

A client file
confidential, inside the company
→
Copy-paste
into a personal AI chat
→
Someone else's servers
outside the company, maybe abroad
→
Out of your hands
may be stored, read, or used to train the AI
📎

Think of it like this: photocopying a confidential file and leaving the copy at the corner print shop. Once it's out, you can't pull it back.

WHAT CAN GO WRONG

Four risks, in plain words

Secrets leak out
Client data, code, contracts leave the building.
Wrong answers slip through
AI can invent facts. Nobody checks what nobody sees.
Rules get broken
Privacy laws (like GDPR in Europe) and client contracts still apply.
No trace, no help
If something goes wrong, nobody knows what was shared, or where.
1 in 5
companies studied had a data breach linked to shadow AI.
+$670K
extra cost per breach, on average, when shadow AI is widespread.

WHEN IT MADE THE NEWS

It already happened

Samsung, 2023: three leaks in 20 days
1
March 11. Samsung lets its chip engineers use ChatGPT.
2
Less than 20 days later. Three slips: secret code pasted to fix a bug, a chip test sequence to "optimize", a recorded meeting turned into notes.
3
First fix. A cap on how much text each question can hold.
4
May. Public AI tools are banned on work devices.
New York, 2023
The lawyer and the six fake cases
A lawyer asked ChatGPT for past court rulings. It invented six of them, with names and quotes. Nobody checked. The judge fined the lawyers $5,000.
Summer 2025
The chats that landed on Google
One small checkbox, "make this chat discoverable", and 4,500+ shared ChatGPT conversations showed up in Google, some with work details. OpenAI removed the option.
THE FIX

Don't ban it. Bring it into the light.

🚫 Just ban it
People keep using it, on their phone or at home. The shadow gets bigger.
💡 Light it up
Offer an approved AI tool, set simple rules, teach people. The shadow shrinks.

The simplest rule to remember: a traffic light.

Public info
A press release, a public web page → OK in any AI tool.
Internal info
Team notes, internal docs → only in the company's approved AI tool.
Sensitive info
Client data, passwords, health data, source code → never in a personal AI account.
KEY TAKEAWAY

The one thing to remember

Shadow AI is a signal, not a crime: it shows people need AI. The answer is to give them a safe, approved way to use it.
