THE BASIC IDEA

An AI that doesn't talk. It decides.

Jev was released on September 15, 2026 by TypeSafe AI. It is not a chatbot and not an LLM: it is the first model of a new family of foundation models called System One models, built for automation.

A NEW KIND OF MODEL

Not an LLM. A new kind of foundation model.

A foundation model is a big, general AI model trained once and reused for many tasks. GPT, Claude and Gemini are foundation models of one type: LLMs. Jev is a foundation model of a different type, with a different architecture.

LLM · "autoregressive"
Predicts the next word, adds it, predicts again. One loop per word. Output: free text a human reads.
System One model · Jev
Skips word-by-word generation. One single pass. Output: typed answers and probabilities that software uses directly.
WHERE THE NAME COMES FROM

Why "System One"?

The name comes from psychologist Daniel Kahneman and his book Thinking, Fast and Slow. He splits human thinking into two modes.

System 1: fast
Instant, intuitive, automatic.
Catching a ball. Braking when a car cuts in. Knowing a face is angry.
System 2: slow
Deliberate, step by step, effortful.
Writing an essay. Solving 17 × 24. Planning a trip.

LLMs are good at System 2 work: writing, reasoning, explaining. Jev is built for System 1 work: the thousands of small, fast judgment calls inside software.

UNDER THE HOOD

Four steps, one pass

  1. Input: a "state"
    A snapshot of what's going on, as text or JSON: an email, an agent's plan and pending command, a game frame described as data.
  2. Typed questions
    You define the answer format in advance. Three primitives: Yes/No (a probability), Choice (one option from a list), Score (a number on a scale). Many questions run in parallel on the same state.
  3. One single pass
    No word-by-word loop. The model evaluates the state and scores every allowed answer at once. That's where the speed comes from, and why output is free: nothing is generated.
  4. Output: typed values + confidence
    The answer can only be one of the values you allowed. No free text to parse, no invented category. This is what TypeSafe means by "can't hallucinate".

The training trick: calibration. Jev is trained with a method TypeSafe calls RLCD, reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions: probabilities are scored against real outcomes, not against what human raters prefer. When Jev says 90%, it should be right about 9 times out of 10. That's what lets you set a rule like "act alone above 90%, ask a human below".

3
The name. A nod to economist William Stanley Jevons. In 1865 he noticed that more efficient steam engines made Britain burn more coal, not less: when a resource gets cheaper, we use much more of it. TypeSafe's bet: cheap decisions will end up everywhere.
KEEP IN MIND

Where it stops

"Can't hallucinate" ≠ always right
It can't leave your format, but it can pick the wrong option.
Doesn't write
For drafting, summarising or open reasoning, you still need an LLM.
Text and JSON only
No images or audio as input, for now.
KEY TAKEAWAY

The one thing to remember

LLMs talk. Jev decides: one fast pass, a typed answer, a calibrated probability. It doesn't replace LLMs, it takes over the small calls they were too slow and too costly for.
