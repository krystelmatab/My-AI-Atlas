THE BIG SHIFT

Three ways we tried to teach machines

Imagine teaching a child what a dog is. AI history is basically three answers to that question.

1950s to 1980s
1. Write the rules

"Four legs, fur, barks." Humans type every rule by hand.

Breaks when reality is messy: what about a dog with three legs?

1990s to 2010s
2. Show examples

Show 10,000 photos labeled "dog" or "cat". The machine finds the pattern itself.

Called machine learning: learning from data instead of rules.

2017 to today
3. Let it read everything

Give it most of the internet and one game: guess the next word.

To guess well, it has to pick up grammar, facts and reasoning along the way.

WHY NOW?

Why it suddenly took off: a recipe with 3 ingredients

The ideas were old (neural networks date from the 1950s). What changed after 2010 is that all three ingredients finally showed up at the same time.

Data. The internet turned the world into billions of photos and pages to learn from.
Chips. Graphics cards built for video games turned out to be perfect for AI math.
Ideas. Better recipes for training, then the Transformer in 2017.
TODAY

Where AI stands in 2026

AI is now a teenager: impressive, fast-growing, and still not always reliable.

It can

Write, translate, code, read images, talk out loud, and carry out multi-step tasks with tools ("agents").

It still

Makes things up with total confidence (called hallucinations), and doesn't always know when it's wrong.

We're asking

Who checks it? What happens to jobs? Laws are arriving: the EU AI Act entered into force in August 2024.

Every AI "summer" promised machines as smart as us. Every winter came when promises ran ahead of reality.

Are we in a summer that lasts, or before a third winter? Nobody knows for sure.
